Les Traboules de Lyon: passageways behind ordinary doors

Imagine walking along a street in Lyon and noticing an ordinary doorway. Where public access is permitted, the route beyond it may lead through a building and into a quiet courtyard before emerging on another street. This is the surprise of a traboule: a connection you cannot always guess from the pavement.

Traboules are passageways linking streets through one or more buildings. Courtyards can form part of the route, with stone walls and an opening to the sky creating a striking change from the street outside. Not every courtyard is a through route, so the words describe related spaces rather than exactly the same thing.

The passages of the Croix-Rousse area are closely linked to Lyon's silk workers, known as canuts. Their story belongs to the working life of the city, not only its architecture. You do not need to learn a long timeline to appreciate the practical idea of a route through a block rather than around it.

Some traboules are accessible to visitors today, but the buildings are also people's homes. Follow access notices, speak quietly, leave private areas alone and never force a closed door. Three words help you follow the scene: la porte means door, le passage means passageway, and la cour means courtyard. Imagine moving from one to the next, then looking up at the sky. The discovery is richer when you remember that this remarkable setting is also an everyday place to live.
