Bonjour {{first_name}}, ✈️

The plane has landed. You pick up your cabin bag, step into the terminal, and French is suddenly more than something on a page. Start with the signs: Arrivées means arrivals. Your first useful word is already there.

If your route takes you through passport control, have your documents ready and follow the staff's directions. A simple Bonjour is enough to begin an exchange. There is no need to prepare a speech.

Next, look for Bagages, meaning baggage. Need a little help? Try: Où sont les bagages, s'il vous plaît ? Where is baggage claim, please?

You may also see Correspondances, meaning connections. If Paris is your destination, look for Sortie, the exit, rather than following connecting passengers.

Bag collected, you are ready for the next question: Où sont les taxis ? Where are the taxis? Or: Où est la gare ? Where is the station?

Keep these questions handy for the terminal. You can ask someone to repeat an answer, check which way to go, and begin your first day in France with a little French.
