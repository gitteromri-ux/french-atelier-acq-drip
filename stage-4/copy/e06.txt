Bonjour {{first_name}}, 🎄

In Strasbourg, lights hang above winter streets and timber-framed buildings, with the cathedral rising beyond the market stalls. Wooden chalets and the scent of cinnamon give Christmas here a distinctly Alsatian setting.

Pause at the food rather than rushing to the next square. Bredele are small Christmas biscuits; pain d'épices is spiced gingerbread. You might choose a bretzel, the local pretzel, when you want something savoury instead.

The French words fit easily into the scene. Les lumières are the lights, un marché de Noël is a Christmas market, and les épices are spices. You can practise simply by naming what catches your attention.

If you want to ask about a biscuit, try: Qu'est-ce que c'est ? What is it? To order one, say: Un, s'il vous plaît. One, please.

A greeting at a stall, a question about a pastry, a thank you before you leave: small exchanges let you experience Christmas in French. Keep walking towards the cathedral, with a few new words to recognise along the way.
