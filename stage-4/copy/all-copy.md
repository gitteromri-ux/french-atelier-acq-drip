Bonjour {{first_name}}, 🌊

In Brittany, a goodbye might sound a little different: kenavo. It is Breton, not a regional way of speaking French. And that small word introduces a part of France with a Celtic language of its own.

Bretagne is the French name for Brittany. In Breton, it is Breizh. The language belongs to the same family as Welsh and Cornish, which helps explain why some local names look unlike the French you already know.

Then there is kouign-amann. You may recognise the pastry before the word: folded dough, plenty of butter and a caramelised edge. Its Breton name means butter cake, and it has travelled well beyond its home region.

Try this simple question in French: Vous connaissez la Bretagne ? Do you know Brittany? You can follow it with J'aimerais y aller, meaning I would like to go there.

A pastry name, a greeting, a place on a sign: each offers a small introduction to Breton culture. French gives you a way to ask about the stories behind them.

### Landing page

#### Two names for the same region

Start with a name you can recognise twice: Bretagne in French, Breizh in Breton. Seeing both is a useful reminder that the language of a place can tell you something different from its national address. You are still in France, but another linguistic history is present. Breton belongs to the Celtic family, alongside Welsh and Cornish, rather than being a form of French.

#### Three words to take with you

Kenavo means goodbye. Keep it as a word to recognise rather than assuming everyone you meet speaks Breton. Breizh is worth remembering when you notice the abbreviation BZH on a sticker. Kouign-amann means butter cake, a remarkably direct name for a pastry made with dough, butter and sugar. The cake is associated with Douarnenez, where its preparation is a local point of pride. Here, a regional word can become familiar to French speakers through something they eat.

#### French helps you ask about Breton

You do not need to choose between being curious about Breton and practising French. Use French to ask a straightforward question: Comment dit-on bonjour en breton ? How do you say hello in Breton? Or point to a word and ask: Qu'est-ce que cela veut dire ? What does that mean? The exchange gives someone space to explain rather than asking you to perform a perfect local accent.

#### Read a place more carefully

If you see a bilingual sign, pause over the two versions. Which letters change? Can you spot a familiar place name? Do not expect the words to match one for one. This is a small exercise in noticing, not a test. The useful discovery is that French culture includes regional voices, and a little French can help you ask better questions about them.

What makes a dress memorable: how it looks, or how it lets you move? Gabrielle Chanel made comfort central to her approach, using soft fabrics and practical shapes rather than treating elegance as something stiff.

One important choice was jersey, a knitted fabric associated with underwear and sportswear. She used its suppleness for clothes that could feel relaxed while still looking carefully considered.

Her little black dress offered another useful idea: one simple garment, changed by the way you wear it. Chanel was not the only designer working with black, but her versions helped make it a lasting part of the modern wardrobe.

Three French words make this easier to describe: une robe is a dress, le tissu is fabric, and confortable means comfortable. Try: Cette robe est confortable. This dress is comfortable.

Her design influence is one part of a more complicated life. Looking closely at the clothes gives you something specific to explore: how fabric, cut and movement changed the experience of getting dressed.

### Landing page

#### Begin with the fabric

Look at a garment before looking at its label. Does it hold a rigid shape, or fall softly around the body? Chanel's use of knitted jersey matters because the material was already practical, supple and familiar in underwear and sportswear. Applying it to fashionable clothing changed the context, not the basic nature of the fabric. A useful French phrase is un tissu souple, a supple fabric.

#### Notice what the wearer can do

Comfort is a specific design problem: can you move your arms, sit down and carry on with your day? Chanel's approach repeatedly returned to freedom of movement. Her softer, cardigan-like suit jackets are another example of that concern. When looking at a photograph, ask yourself where the garment gives the wearer room. The French word la coupe means the cut. The appeal was not that nobody had worn black before. Chanel helped make a simple black dress a flexible wardrobe choice, adaptable through accessories and styling. A designer's influence is not a complete account of their life, and this is not a biography. Nor does one designer stand for all French style. Looking closely at individual garments is a more useful starting point. Choose a photograph, identify the fabric or cut, and say what you notice in one short French sentence. You do not need fashion expertise to begin.

Bonjour {{first_name}}, 📚

A crown is the obvious place to begin with Charlemagne. A book is a more revealing one. Alongside his rule, he supported an effort to improve learning, train clergy and make written texts more reliable.

People across a large realm needed to read, teach and interpret religious texts. Better Latin and carefully corrected books were part of the answer. Across a fragmented medieval landscape, shared texts and teaching helped connect the regions of his realm. Learning was connected to both faith and authority.

Scholars came to his court from different regions, bringing knowledge that did not begin with him. The cultural revival associated with his reign built on older traditions rather than creating education from nothing.

A few French words bring the setting into focus: un manuscrit is a handwritten document, un monastère is a monastery, and un empereur is an emperor.

Charlemagne is not simply the founder of France. He belongs to a wider medieval history, where language, religion and power meet. Learning French offers another way to approach that history, from a manuscript's description to the questions you ask in a museum.

À bientôt,
The French Atelier team

### Landing page

#### A shared problem of understanding

Errors in a handwritten book could make a passage harder to teach; unfamiliar Latin could make it harder to understand. For Charlemagne, improving education was closely connected to improving religious life. Clergy needed stronger Latin to read and interpret scripture. Corrected texts and teaching were therefore practical concerns, not simply signs of a cultivated court.

#### An exchange of knowledge

The scholars who gathered around him brought traditions from elsewhere. Alcuin came from York; other teachers and writers arrived from Italy, Ireland and beyond. Their presence matters because it prevents the story becoming a tale of one man inventing learning. The court drew on knowledge that other communities had preserved and developed. Charlemagne's support helped bring that work together and give it institutional backing.

#### Schools, books and authority

Teaching in monasteries and bishoprics included grammar, calculation and religious material. Books had to be copied by hand, making the care taken over a text especially important. The educational effort was also tied to governing a large realm through shared religious and learned practices. This was not universal schooling in the modern sense. It served particular institutions and groups, even as its influence extended beyond the court.

#### Read the history in French

Three words can help you begin with a museum label: un manuscrit, a manuscript; un monastère, a monastery; and un empereur, an emperor. Try this question: À quoi servait ce manuscrit ? What was this manuscript used for? It directs attention to the people who read, copied and used an object. Learning French gives you another way to approach those questions, without reducing centuries of history to a single ruler or a single origin story.

Pause at the food rather than rushing to the next square. Bredele are small Christmas biscuits; pain d'épices is spiced gingerbread. You might choose a bretzel, the local pretzel, when you want something savoury instead.

### Landing page

#### Look up before you look at the stalls

Begin with the city around the market. Strasbourg's cathedral, the timber-framed buildings of Petite France and the illuminated streets give the celebrations their setting. Let that shape your imagined walk: notice a façade, a bridge or a view before choosing where to pause. Les lumières means the lights.
