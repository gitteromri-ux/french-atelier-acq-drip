How French Atelier works

What makes French Atelier different.

Apps drill you. People teach you.

Plenty of apps will teach you French words. Very few will teach you to actually speak French with another person. That gap is where French Atelier lives.

Most language apps ask you to drill alone, tapping through exercises with no one listening back. That method builds recognition, but it rarely builds confidence in a real conversation, because a screen cannot hear your accent or respond to your hesitation.

French Atelier works the other way. Classes are live, taught by real teachers broadcasting from France, in small groups of 8 to 12 learners, for 85 minutes at a time. You speak, someone corrects you, and the next sentence comes out a little more sure than the last.

At the end of each level, you earn a CEFR certificate, the same standard framework used by schools and employers across Europe to describe language ability. It is a marker of real progress, not just a streak counter.

The courses themselves are backed by Acadomia, France's leading education group, built by an organization with decades in classroom teaching, not a startup guessing at pedagogy for the first time.

Between live classes, Julien, the in-platform AI French companion, answers up to five questions a day, included with every course, so a quick doubt never has to wait for the next session.

None of this means solo apps are without value. They are a fine way to memorize vocabulary. They are simply not built to teach you to hold a conversation, because that skill only grows through real back and forth with another person.

If speaking with real confidence is the goal, see how a live class actually works.
